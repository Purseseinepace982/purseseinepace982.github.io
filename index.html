🛡️ Giolit-SVA-Verifier - Prove Your Assertions Hold Forever

Giolit-SVA-Verifier reads the rules you wrote about your chip design — they are called SystemVerilog assertions — and answers one big question: do those rules hold for every possible input? If the answer is yes, you receive a signed proof certificate and a safety margin you can hand to a reviewer. If the answer is no, you receive the exact cycle that breaks your rule, so you know precisely where to look.

🎯 What This Tool Actually Does

Most design checking works by trying a large number of example situations and hoping the important ones are covered. Giolit-SVA-Verifier does something different. It examines the rule you wrote and reasons about all of the situations that could ever occur — including the strange ones a person would never think to test by hand.

There are only two possible outcomes, and both are useful:

  • Everything holds. You get a signed proof certificate, a document that says the rule was checked exhaustively, plus a safety margin that tells you how much room you have before the result becomes fragile.
  • Something breaks. You get the exact cycle — the precise moment in time — where the design violates the rule, along with the sequence of inputs that leads there.

📦 What the Trial Includes

The trial is not a demo video and it is not a slideshow. It ships the real pieces, so you can run the verifier on your own material and judge the results yourself.

  • sva-verify — the verifier itself. This is the component that takes an assertion and determines whether it holds for every input or produces the failing cycle.
  • SDK — the software development kit. It provides the building blocks that let you automate the verifier and connect it to the rest of your flow.
  • Benchmarks — a set of worked examples with known outcomes, so you can watch the tool succeed and watch it catch a genuine failure before you trust it with your own design.
  • Evidence — the supporting material that shows the results, so a reviewer or a teammate can inspect what happened instead of taking your word for it.
  • Proof certificates and safety margins — the signed outputs you keep, archive, and attach to a review package.

🔍 How It Works, In Plain English

  1. You describe the promise. Your design carries statements like “this signal must never be high at the same time as that one.” In SystemVerilog these statements are called assertions, and they are written in the design language itself. Giolit-SVA-Verifier reads them as they are.
  2. The verifier explores everything. Instead of running a handful of tests, it reasons about every combination of inputs the design could ever receive, across every cycle of time that matters. This is the branch of engineering called formal verification.
  3. You get a verdict you can act on. A pass arrives as a signed certificate with a stated margin. A fail arrives as a concrete cycle, with the trail of inputs that leads to it.

🧾 Signed Proof Certificates and Safety Margins

A plain “pass” message is not very useful six months later, when the person who ran the check has moved to another team. That is why the result is packaged as a certificate. The certificate records what was checked, how it was checked, and it is signed, so anyone can confirm it was produced by the tool and has not been altered afterwards.

The safety margin is the companion number. It tells you how far your design sits from the edge of the condition being checked. A generous margin means the result is robust. A thin margin is an early warning, even though the check technically passed — and that warning is often worth more than the pass itself.

📊 Benchmarks and Evidence

Claims about verification tools are easy to make and hard to check. The benchmark set included with the trial is designed so you can check them yourself. Each example has a known correct outcome, so a pass that should be a fail — or the reverse — is immediately visible.

🧰 The SDK and Automation

Running one check by hand is fine for a first look. Running a thousand checks every night is what a real project needs. The SDK is the part that makes the second option possible.

  • Call the verifier from your own scripts so a whole list of assertions runs in one go.
  • Collect the certificates and margins as files, ready to archive alongside a release.
  • Feed the failing cycles straight back to the person who owns the design, with no manual translation step in between.

🏗️ Who This Is For

Giolit-SVA-Verifier is aimed at teams that cannot afford to find out about a design flaw late. That includes people building integrated circuits and field-programmable gate arrays, and people working on systems where a mistake is measured in harm rather than in money — automotive control units, medical devices, industrial machinery, and anything else where a silent failure is unacceptable.

It is also for the reviewers standing behind those teams. A signed certificate with a stated safety margin is something a reviewer can verify. A verbal assurance from a busy engineer is not.

💻 Running It on Windows

The application is intended for Windows. After you have downloaded it from the release page, keep it in the folder you created in the getting-started steps. Launching it belongs to the same routine every time: open that folder, start the application, and point it at the files you want checked.

If Windows shows a first-run prompt about an unknown application, that is the standard protection feature for any software downloaded from the internet. The same prompt appears for almost every tool distributed outside a store, and it is resolved the usual way before the verifier starts.

❓ Common Questions

Do I need to know how to write code?

To download and start the application, no. To interpret a certificate or a failing cycle, you will want someone familiar with the design — that person is usually the one who wrote the assertion in the first place.

What if my assertion is correct but the margin is small?

Treat the small margin as a genuine signal. It means your design works, but with little room to spare. That is exactly the situation worth discussing before the design is frozen.

What does the trial actually let me do?

It gives you the verifier, the SDK, the benchmark set, and the evidence material. That is enough to reproduce the published results, run the samples on your own machine, and see the certificate format for yourself.

Can I try it before committing to anything? That is what the trial is for. Run it on the benchmarks first, then on a rule you already understand well. The result will tell you more than any description could.
